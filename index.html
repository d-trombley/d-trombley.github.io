I build AI workflows for businesses. Both demos below are live, run on made-up or published data, and were graded against an answer key.

  1. Invoice agent: checks emailed invoices against the PO and receipt. Live demo.
  2. Cash receipts matching: applies bank deposits to open invoices. Live demo.

Invoice agent

Open the live demo. Made-up vendors and invoices, no sign-in.

Checks every emailed invoice against its PO and receipt. Clean invoices go to a person's approve list, and only the flagged ones need a closer look.

  • Results: in a full run with Claude on a 43-invoice test set, every field was read correctly, all 16 planted problems were caught, including two phishing emails, and there were 0 false flags. About 2.5 cents and 4 seconds per invoice. 137 automated tests.
  • How it works: Claude reads each PDF and returns the fields with a confidence score. Code does the math in whole cents, applies the 2% price tolerance, catches duplicates and checks every vendor and PO against the ledger.
  • Exceptions: unsure fields and every flag (price over PO, short shipment, duplicate, changed bank details, a look-alike or forged sender) go to a queue. A flagged invoice needs a written reason to approve.
  • Controls: Claude can't approve anything. A new vendor needs one person to ask and a different person to approve, with the bank details confirmed by phone. Every step is logged with its inputs, the model output and its cost.
  • Next: sign-in with roles, a second approver above a set amount, and a weekly accuracy check.

Cash receipts matching

Open the live demo. Published accounting data with made-up messy cases, no sign-in.

Applies bank deposits to open invoices and builds the journal entry. A clerk works the exceptions and a controller approves the batch.

  • Results: October had 129 bank lines, 204 payments and 802 invoice applications (one payment can pay several invoices). The rules applied 583 of the 802 automatically (72.7%) with 0 wrong, 801 of 802 (99.9%) were right after review, and the journal entry ties to the answer key to the cent.
  • How it works: rules apply the payments they can prove and suggest a match for most of the rest. Claude is asked only about payments the rules can't place, like an unknown payer or two customers with the same name. It looks up open invoices and customers with read-only tools. Code checks every answer, and one that fails goes to exceptions with the reason.
  • Claude's part: in October, Claude was asked about 3 of 204 payments. It placed one across 6 invoices, all correct. It said it couldn't tell on the other two (two customers with the same name, and a payment with no payer), so they went to a person. About 8 cents a payment. The demo asks Claude live, with a daily spending cap.
  • Controls: the clerk accepts or edits each suggestion. Claude can't apply, approve or post anything. Clean matches still go into the batch the controller approves.
  • Testing: a score page grades each measure against the answer key, split by rules, Claude and person, and lists every miss. 135 automated tests.
  • Test data: 129 bank lines and 1,127 open invoices from one month of a published accounting dataset, with fees, short pays, payer name variants and missing remittances added.
